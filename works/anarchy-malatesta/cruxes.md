# Cruxes in Anarchy

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[AN II.31.5] government as delegation of power`.

This is an English-only build: the Italian original is still to come (see SOURCES.md). Those below that need the Italian to settle them say so.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `II.76.3` | "Human progress is measured by the degree in which government and private property are administered." The argument of the whole paragraph (`II.76.2`: "the less there will be of government and of private property") needs "diminished" or "reduced", not "administered". Probably a slip in the translation or the printing. The historical column keeps it; check the Italian and a scan, then write the draft from the sense and add a note. This is the book's closing argument, so it matters. |
| `0.12.1` to `0.18.1` | Why Malatesta prefers "abolition of government" to "abolition of the State". Readers who know Marx and Lenin on the withering away of the state will want to compare. The draft must keep "government" and "State" apart as he does (see the glossary); the comparison belongs in `commentary/`. |
| `II.31.1` to `II.31.6` | Government against administration: "delegation of power" against "delegation of work". The core of the anarchist answer to "who will run things?". Every tradition will read it its own way; settle the wording here first. |
| `II.49.1` to `II.49.11` | The two methods Malatesta rejects, "State Socialist" and "Individualist". "Individualism ... is in theory a kind of Anarchy without Socialism" (`II.49.7`). A modern reader will map this onto today's libertarians and today's state socialists; whether that mapping holds is for commentary, not for the draft. |
| `II.54.1` to `II.54.3` | "Anarchy is synonymous with Socialism", were it not for "that school of Socialists" who divide the social question. Which school he means needs a note; most likely the parliamentary socialists of his day, but check before saying so. |
| `II.11.1` and `II.56.1` | "Authoritarians" here means socialists who want to keep a state, not dictators. The modern draft or a note must make that clear, or the argument reads as aimed at the wrong people. |
| `II.37.1` to `II.37.6` | Crime and defence: the "delinquent" as "a sick brother in need of cure". The 1900 English rests on the science of its day ("a man's character is the consequence of his physical organism", `II.37.2`). Keep the argument against punishment; mark the dated science in a note rather than in the draft. |
| `I.9.1` to `I.10.3` | The struggle for existence and co-operation, with a swipe at the "Darwinian theory, which the bourgeois class have ridden to death". Kropotkin was making the same case in the articles that became *Mutual Aid* (MA in this library); a cross-reference belongs here once MA is in. |
| `II.62.1` to `II.64.3` | Malatesta quotes Bakunin on organization and influence. The source is named only as "an article published in 1872". Find it, check the quotation against Bakunin's French, and add the reference in a note. |
| `0.43.4` and `II.27.2` | Quotations from Kropotkin and Sismondi, given in English with no source. Find the originals; the wording here is the translator's, not theirs. |
