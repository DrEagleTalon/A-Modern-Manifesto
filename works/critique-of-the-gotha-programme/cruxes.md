# Cruxes in the Critique of the Gotha Programme

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[CGP I.56.1] from each according to ability`.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `I.3.1` to `I.3.7` | "Labour is not the source of all wealth. Nature is just as much the source of use values." A short argument that the rest of the notes lean on. Keep "Gebrauchswerte" (use values) as a term, not "useful things". |
| `I.10.3` | "und der Mensch ist Wilder, nachdem er aufgehört hat, Affe zu sein". The mlwerke.de copy reads "ein Wilder". The words "Wilder" and "Affe" are of their time; translate them plainly and leave a note, do not soften them into something Marx did not say. |
| `I.33.2` to `I.50.2` | The deductions from the social product (I.36 to I.46). The list must stay a list, and "verkürzt" in I.48.1 must echo "unverkürzt" in I.33.2. See the glossary. |
| `I.51.1` to `I.56.1` | The two phases of communist society, the "birthmarks" of the old society and the "equal right" that is still bourgeois. The most cited pages of the work. Translate exactly; the arguments over "socialism" as a name for the first phase belong in `commentary/`. |
| `I.56.1` | "Jeder nach seinen Fähigkeiten, jedem nach seinen Bedürfnissen!" One long sentence of four "nachdem" clauses before the slogan. Variant `T10`: in 1891 "Produktivkräfte" reads "die Produktionskräfte". |
| `I.59.5` | "hat es von den bürgerlichen Ökonomen überkommen" (inherited from); the mlwerke.de copy reads "übernommen" (taken over). Close in sense, but check a printed MEW 19. |
| `I.60.1` to `I.69.2` | "Only one reactionary mass." Marx quotes the Manifesto (CM I.47.1 to I.48.4 in this library) against Lassalle. Cross-cite the CM verses in `notes`. I.69.2 ("der Berliner Marat") is a jibe at Wilhelm Hasselmann; a note should say who he was. |
| `II.4.1` to `II.4.7` | The iron law of wages, Goethe's "ewigen, ehernen, großen Gesetzen", Friedrich Albert Lange and Malthus. Needs notes, not paraphrase. |
| `II.6.1` to `II.7.1` | Wages as the price of labour power, and the slave who writes on the programme of the revolt that slavery must go because the keep of slaves cannot rise above a low maximum. The image carries the argument; keep it whole. |
| `III.1.1` to `III.7.2` | Producers' cooperatives with state aid. Marx rejects state-founded cooperatives and values those workers build on their own (III.7.2). Readers from cooperative and mutual-aid traditions will care about this line; keep it sharp. |
| `IV.5.3` | Freedom is turning the state from an organ set above society into one wholly beneath it. A sentence anarchist and Marxist readers both claim. Neutral wording here, the readings in `commentary/`. |
| `IV.11.1` to `IV.11.2` | The "revolutionary dictatorship of the proletariat", the only place in the work where the phrase stands. See the glossary. |
| `IV.13.1` to `IV.15.1` | The democratic demands, the democratic republic and the "Militärdespotismus" of the Prussian-German Empire. IV.15.1 is one very long sentence with two passages Engels cut in 1891 and variants `T15` and `T16`. |
| `IV.20.1` to `IV.23.4` | Education by the state. Marx wants schools paid for and inspected by law but government and church kept out of the school. Keep "Volkserziehung durch den Staat" distinct from public funding. |
| `IV.26.1` to `IV.26.3` | Freedom of conscience. Marx asks that each may meet "his religious as well as his bodily needs" without the police, then says the workers' party wants to free consciences from "religious spookery". Variant `T17`: in 1891 Engels softened "religiöse ... Notdurft" to "religiösen ... Bedürfnisse". Translate what Marx wrote, give the 1891 reading, and leave the argument about it to `commentary/`. |
| `B.4.1`, `B.5.2`, `B.8.5`, `B.10.1` to `B.10.5`, `II.8.1`, `III.3.1`, `IV.15.1`, `IV.42.1` | The passages in `< >` that Engels left out in 1891 (P1891.4.1 says why). Decide once how the modern draft and the reader show them, and apply it everywhere. |
| `IV.43.1` | "Dixi et salvavi animam meam." Latin, after Ezekiel 3:19. Keep the Latin with a note. |
