# Sources: The Civil War in France (CWF)

## Original column (English)

Marx wrote all three addresses of the General Council of the International Working Men's Association in English. The First Address on the Franco-Prussian War is dated 23 July 1870 and the Second 9 September 1870; both came out as leaflets in London. The Civil War in France was read to the General Council on 30 May 1871, two days after the last barricades fell, and printed in London in June 1871 with two notes at the end. All of this is in the public domain.

**Text used:** the Marxists Internet Archive transcription of the 1871 English edition (mark-up by Zodiac and Brian Baggins, proofed and corrected by Matthew Carmody 2009; MIA transcriptions are CC BY-SA), files `ch01.html` to `ch06.html` and `news.html` from the mirror at `github.com/MARX-EBOOKS/marx-ebooks.github.io`, path `en/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/`. MIA spells "labor", "honor" and the like the American way in most places and keeps "defence" and "favour"; that mix is MIA's, not Marx's, and is left as it is.

**Left out:**

- MIA's own introduction (`intro.html`), notes on publication (taken from MECW 22, which is protected), picture page and index.
- MIA's chapter titles in square brackets ("[The Paris Commune]" and so on) and its endnotes.
- MIA's square-bracket glosses inside the text, 40 in all, logged in `sources/clean/mia_insertions_removed.json`. Three bracketed years that the library's MIA reader keeps ("[1792]", "[1848]" twice) and the date "[19]" are removed through the corrections below. Marx's own "[!]" in `IV.34.1` and "(?)" in `II.10.3` stay.
- MIA's "[...]" lines between quoted passages in the First Address.
- MIA's English of Engels's 1891 introduction (`postscript.html`): it is abridged (the whole narrative of 1870 and 1871 is missing) and its translator is not named, so its rights are unclear.
- The footnote on "tickets-of-leave" that MIA prints in part I. It is Engels's note to the German edition of 1871 (MEW 17, marked "Anmerkung von Engels zur deutschen Ausgabe von 1871") and is not in the English original.

## P1891: Engels's introduction (German)

Engels wrote the introduction to the third German edition (Berlin 1891) in German, dated London, 18 March 1891. This section's original column holds that German and its English column is empty, as in Principles of Communism; the modern draft is translated from the German. Verses carry MEW page numbers.

**Text:** MEW 22, pp. 188-199 (Dietz, Berlin), via the mlwerke.de mirror at `github.com/wertform/mlwerke.de` (`me22/me22_188.htm`). MEW's one editorial insertion, "[Darboy]" in `P1891.15.5`, is kept in square brackets. The rule of dashes MEW prints between Engels's account of the events and his assessment (after `P1891.15`) has no words and is not a verse.

**Also fetched, not used:** MEW 17, pp. 319-365 (`me17_319.htm`), Engels's German translation of the 1871 Address. It is a translation and this work has no column for it. It is a good check on how Engels read Marx's English, and the cruxes below point to it.

The order is that of the 1891 edition. Paragraph 0 of each section is a heading. The headings of `A1` and `A2` follow MIA's ("The First Address, July 23, 1870"); the headings of `I` to `IV` and `N1` `N2` are the bare numerals of the 1871 printing. Signatures (the closing cry of `A2`, the General Council's names at the end of `IV`, John Hales's letter in `N2`, Engels's date line) are verses of kind `signature` and are not split.
