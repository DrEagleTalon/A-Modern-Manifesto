# Cruxes in The Conquest of Bread

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[CB III.27.2] the Communism of the Free`.

This is an English-only build: the French original is still to come (see SOURCES.md). Those below that need the French to settle them say so.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `III.27.2` | "Anarchist Communism, Communism without government, the Communism of the Free." The book's own definition of its aim. Every tradition in the library will want to read it its own way; settle the wording here and put the readings in `commentary/`. |
| `II.40.1` to `II.44.2` | "The right to well-being" against "the right to work". In US English "right to work" now names anti-union laws, so a modern reader can take the passage backwards. Needs a note at `II.40.1` (see the glossary). |
| `II.41.1` and `II.41.2` | "1s. 8d. a day". The translator gives the national workshops' pay of 1848 in English money. Keep the historical sum in the historical column; any modern equivalent belongs in a note, not in the draft. The same holds for the prices in `XVII.75.1` to `XVII.75.5`. |
| `V.63.1` to `V.67.3` | The distribution rule: free use of what is plentiful, equal rations of what is scarce. "Rations" now suggests wartime hardship, which is not Kropotkin's point. Check the French wording. |
| `XIII.6.2` to `XIII.20.2` | Kropotkin against the labour-cheques of the Collectivists. A modern reader may hear "collectivist" as a general word for socialism; it named a definite current. Notes, and a cross-reference to Marx's Gotha critique once CGP is in the library. |
| `P1913.13.3` | "The mass deportations to New Guinea" after June 1848. The insurgents of 1848 were transported to Algeria, and those of 1851 also to Cayenne; New Guinea looks wrong. Check whether the slip is Kropotkin's or the printing's (and whether the preface was written in English at all) before writing a note. |
| `IV.59.2` and `XVI.22.7` | "New Guinea savages", "Hindoo workmen". Dated and colonial language. The historical column keeps it; the modern draft should say what Kropotkin means without the slur, and a note can mark the change. In `XVI.22.7` his point is that Indian workers are not inferior. |
| `X.24.1` to `X.26.3` | Housework and women's labour. Kropotkin argues that freeing women from domestic drudgery is part of the revolution. The 1906 English is stiff here ("woman must consider herself a slave"); a modern draft needs care not to flatten the argument. |
| `IX.2.1` to `IX.3.2` | "The need for luxury". Kropotkin means art and leisure, what makes life more than eating and sleeping, not consumer goods. The word "luxury" will mislead a modern reader unless the draft or a note says so. |
| `XVII.N13.1.1` to `XVII.N13.17.4` | The table for Seine and Seine-et-Oise. The acres and work-days were probably converted from French hectares; check the figures against the French before anyone modernises the units. |
