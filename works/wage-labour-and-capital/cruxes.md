# Cruxes in Wage Labour and Capital

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[WLC I.13.1] labour-power`.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `P1891.5.1` to `P1891.5.2` | Engels explains his one big change: the worker sells labour-power, not labour. Which text does the modern translation follow, and how visible should the 1849 reading be? (Interim answer: follow 1891, show 1849 in notes.) |
| `I.13.1` and every verse with a variant reading "Arbeit" | About 30 places where Engels changed *Arbeit* to *Arbeitskraft*. The variants are attached to each verse. |
| `IV.9.1`, `IV.12.1` | Places where Engels kept "Preis der Arbeit". The 1902 English silently says labour-power anyway. |
| `I.4.1` | Marx's plan of three parts. Only the first was published; the series broke off after instalment V (see `P1891.1.3`). |
| `III.5.1` | "In production, people act not only on nature but on one another." The second half was added in 1891; the 1849 newspaper lacks it. |
| `IV.12.1` to `IV.14.8` | Relative wages: wages can rise while the workers' share falls. The heart of the pamphlet for a modern reader and the hardest passage to make plain. |
| `V.33.4` | "Hecatombs of workers" dragged into the grave in crises. Keep the image or explain it? |
| `III.18.1`, `III.12.3` | Money examples. The German, the 1849 newspaper, Lothrop and MIA all use different currencies. See the glossary entry on currency. |

## Alignment notes

The 1902 English sometimes reorders sentences (for example `P1891.13.2` to `P1891.13.4`), which no automatic alignment can follow. Those verses are flagged `review: true`. Fix them by moving the English text to the right verse.
