# Cruxes in Mutual Aid

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[MA II.50.1] "Don't compete!"`.

The book is an English original, so most cruxes are about what a modern reader will hear in 1902 words, about science that has moved on since, and about passages that need a note rather than a quiet rewrite.

| Verse | Question |
|---|---|
| `P1902.10.3` to `P1902.10.6` | "It is not love and not even sympathy upon which Society is based": it is the sense of human solidarity. Kropotkin's own statement of what mutual aid is and is not. Keep the order of the terms; the rest of the book leans on it. |
| `P1902.14.5` | Kropotkin says the book is about one factor of evolution, not all of them. The draft should keep the modesty of the claim; readers often quote the book as saying more. |
| `I.2.1` and `I.4.2` | Darwin's "large and metaphorical sense" of the struggle for existence against the narrow sense. The whole argument depends on this distinction, so the draft must keep both senses apart and recognisable (see the glossary). |
| `I.6.1` to `I.8.2` | Huxley's "gladiators' show" and "the Hobbesian war of each against all". These are Huxley's words, quoted to be answered. Keep the quotation marks and Huxley's wording; a note can give the essay (The Nineteenth Century, February 1888). |
| `II.50.1` to `II.50.2` | "Don't compete!" Kropotkin puts these words in nature's mouth. The most quoted lines of the book. Keep the imperative. |
| `II.48.2` to `II.48.3` | Kropotkin's argument against selection during calamities. Biology since 1902 (kin selection, reciprocal altruism, multilevel selection) bears on this; that belongs in commentary, not in the draft. |
| `III.0.1` and `IV.0.1` | Chapter titles "Mutual Aid among Savages" and "among the Barbarians". See the glossary. Decide in one Discussion whether the draft keeps the period terms with a note, uses a neutral term, or keeps them only in titles. |
| `III.1.1` | The chapter summary lists peoples by name ("Bushmen and Hottentots", "Papuas", "Aleoutes"). Several of these names are now considered offensive or obsolete (San, Khoikhoi, Unangan). Decide whether the draft modernises the names and how the historical column is annotated. |
| `III.17.1` | "the Hottentots, who are but a little more developed than the Bushmen". A ranking of peoples by "development", typical of 1902 anthropology, inside a passage written to defend them. Keep the claim visible and add a note. |
| `III.33.1` to `III.35.18` | Infanticide, the abandonment of the old ("parricide", `III.34.1`) and cannibalism. Kropotkin explains them as responses to need and duty to the tribe. The draft must keep the line where he draws it, explanation and not approval. |
| `III.30.9` | Veniaminoff's anecdote of the dried fish, four sentences in one verse because the book sets it in brackets. A candidate for splitting with letter suffixes. |
| `IV.20.1` | The Norse ideal of a man, quoted without a source. A note should identify it. |
| `V.19.11` and `V.N38` | The guild tribunal, judged by one's equals. Note 38 is missing from the transcription (see SOURCES.md). |
| `V.25.6` | "the city was a State". Kropotkin praises the medieval city and calls it a State in the same sentence. Readers who know his later writing on the State will want a note on how the two fit. |
| `VII.6.8` | "Science loudly proclaims that the struggle of each against all is the leading principle of nature, and of human societies as well." Kropotkin's target in one sentence. |
| `VII.14.1` | The State destroying the village community in Germany, Austria and Belgium. The historical claims in VII (enclosures, the 1667 edict at `VII.N3`) should be checked in commentary, not changed in the draft. |
| `VIII.22.4` to `VIII.22.5` | The Christian Church "has aided the State in wrecking all standing institutions of mutual aid". Kropotkin separates early Christianity, which he praises, from the Church. Keep the distinction he makes. |
| `VIII.34.1` | "7s. 6d. a week". Old money. The historical column keeps it; the draft can give the sum in words and leave conversions to a note. |
| `C.11.4` to `C.11.7` | The ethical close: Buddhism, Christianity and "some of the Mussulman teachers" named together as sources of the idea of giving more than one receives. Keep each tradition named as he names it. `C.11.7` is the book's last word on mutual support against mutual struggle. |
