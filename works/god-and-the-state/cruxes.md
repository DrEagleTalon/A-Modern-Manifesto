# Cruxes in God and the State

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[GAS 0.70.3] the authority of the bootmaker`.

This is an English-only build: the French original is still to come (see SOURCES.md). Those below that need the French to settle them say so.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `0.121.4` with `0.N5` and `0.N6` | The gap. Three pages of the manuscript are lost in the middle of a sentence, and the editors guess at the missing words in N6. Tucker prints "that do the laws ... they are not exclusively men of science", which does not parse; "that do the laws" may stand for "that is, the laws". The draft must show the gap as a gap and must not paper over it with a smooth sentence. Check the French. |
| `0.70.1` to `0.74.3` | The authority of the bootmaker. The best known passage in the book and the one most quoted against "anarchists reject all expertise". Bakunin separates consulting an expert from obeying one. Settle the wording of "authority" here first (see the glossary); every tradition will read the passage its own way, and those readings belong in `commentary/`. |
| `0.54.1` | "If God really existed, it would be necessary to abolish him", reversing Voltaire's line quoted at `0.23.1` and again at `0.179.5`. The reversal only works if the draft keeps Voltaire's wording recognisable in all three places. |
| `0.5.1` to `0.10.2` | Bakunin reads the story of the Fall as a myth of human emancipation, with Satan as "the first freethinker". The draft has to say what he says, plainly, as his reading of the myth. Readers who hold the text sacred will meet this first; a note can say that it is Bakunin's allegory and how it was received, without arguing for or against it. |
| `P1882.5.2` | "the walls of their cheese". Almost certainly a literal rendering of a French figure, the scholar shut away like a rat in a cheese. Check the French, then draft the sense ("their ivory tower", or keep the image and explain it). |
| `0.11.1` | The elements of human development, each matched to a sphere of life (animality to economy, and so on). The book sets this one paragraph between two rows of asterisks. It is the frame for the whole text, so the draft should keep the numbered form. |
| `0.86.5` to `0.86.6` with `0.N4` | Mazzini's schools and the republic of "men of virtuous genius" (Louis Blanc's phrase in `0.N3.1.2`). Note 4 grows into Bakunin's own programme for education and his account of public opinion. It is long enough to read as a short essay; decide whether the reader shows it inline or as a linked note. |
| `0.120.7` to `0.137.8` | Against government by science. A modern reader will think of technocracy and rule by experts; whether that mapping holds is for commentary, not for the draft. Keep "savant" and "science" as the glossary says. |
| `0.122.2` to `0.128.4` | The rabbit and the vivisection of men. Bakunin fears that a body of scientists left unchecked would experiment on people. Later history gives this weight; any reference to it belongs in a note, not in the draft. |
| `0.151.1` to `0.155.9` and `0.162.8` to `0.162.10` | Bakunin on the Jewish people. Most of it is about Jehovah as a national god, a standard argument of the period. But `0.162.9` and `0.162.10` use ethnic stereotypes ("that exclusive national spirit which distinguishes them even to-day", "that mercantile passion which constitutes one of the principal traits of their character"). Bakunin's hostility to Jews is documented elsewhere in his writing. The draft translates what he wrote, without softening and without sharpening, and the passage needs a plain note that names the stereotype as a stereotype. |
| `0.N9.1.1` to `0.N9.1.4` | "the difference in intelligence existing between races, peoples, and individuals" and men "wholly stupid, foolish even to idiocy". The argument of the note is against the idealists, but it takes the race science of its day for granted. Keep the argument; mark the dated assumption in a note rather than in the draft. |
| `0.150.1` to `0.163.3` and `0.172.2` | "Spiritualism" means the philosophy of pure spirit everywhere except `0.172.2`, where it means seances (see the glossary). Get this wrong and the history of ideas in this part reads as nonsense. |
| `0.17.5`, `0.119.2`, `0.162.5` and `0.N1.1.1` | "the Appendix". Bakunin refers to an appendix that is not in this book, most likely the philosophical appendix to *L'Empire knouto-germanique*. Identify it from the French and add the reference in a note. |
| `0.196.1` to `0.199.2` | The text breaks off mid-attack on Victor Cousin ("the existence of a personal God...."). The French doctrinaire school of Royer-Collard and Cousin needs a short note so a modern reader knows who they were. The draft keeps the abrupt end. |
| `0.18.2` and `0.171.1` | "Credo quod absurdum", attributed to Tertullian. The attribution is traditional and the words are not in Tertullian in that form. Keep the Latin with a translation in the draft, and say so in a note. |
