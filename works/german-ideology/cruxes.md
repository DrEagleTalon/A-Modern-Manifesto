# Cruxes in The German Ideology

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[GI I.41.2] the real movement`.

The work was never finished. Marx and Engels left it as a pile of drafts, fair copies and fragments, and the order of the Feuerbach chapter in MEW is an arrangement made by later editors. The 2017 MEGA volume I/5 prints the manuscripts as separate drafts instead. This library follows MEW and its numbering, but a translator should know that a paragraph break in `I` may be a jump between sheets written months apart.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `I.10.1` to `I.12.2` | "Die Voraussetzungen, mit denen wir beginnen, sind keine willkürlichen." The starting point of the materialist conception of history: real individuals, their activity and their material conditions. Plain and exact; resist turning it into a slogan. |
| `I.26.5` to `I.27.6` | "Das Bewußtsein kann nie etwas Andres sein als das bewußte Sein" and "Nicht das Bewußtsein bestimmt das Leben, sondern das Leben bestimmt das Bewußtsein." The pun on Bewußt-sein cannot be kept in English. See the glossary. |
| `I.36.6` to `I.39.3` | Division of labour, and the hunter, fisherman, herdsman and critic of I.39.3, the most quoted picture of a communist society in the whole of Marx. MEW's text does not show which of the two authors wrote which words of the manuscript. Keep the list as it stands. |
| `I.41.1` to `I.41.5` | "Wir nennen Kommunismus die wirkliche Bewegung, welche den jetzigen Zustand aufhebt." Note the bracketed "[wird]" in I.41.1, a word MEW supplies. See the glossary on aufheben. |
| `I.56.1` to `I.58.6` | "Die Gedanken der herrschenden Klasse sind in jeder Epoche die herrschenden Gedanken." Keep "herrschend" the same word in every clause; the repetition is the argument. |
| `I.106.1` to `I.116.1` | Productive forces against the form of intercourse, Selbstbetätigung against Arbeit. The sentences are long and the terms carry the reasoning. See the glossary on Verkehr and Selbstbetätigung. |
| `I.113.1` | One sentence of four numbered results, among them that a communist revolution is needed both to overthrow the ruling class and so the class overthrowing it can rid itself of "all the old filth". The longest verse in the work. Do not split it in the modern draft; use `notes` to set out the four points if needed. |
| `I.121.4` to `I.121.6` | The illusory community of the state and the real community in which individuals gain their freedom through association. Anarchist and Marxist readers both lean on these lines. Neutral wording here, the readings in `commentary/`. |
| `I.N1`, `P1846.N1` and the other "[Im Manuskript gestrichen:]" notes | Passages the authors struck out. Some (I.N1.3.1, "Wir kennen nur eine einzige Wissenschaft, die Wissenschaft der Geschichte") are as famous as anything in the main text. Translate them in full and keep MEW's label on each. Inside I.N1.1.1 a passage struck a second time sits in round brackets. |
| `AT1.32.1` to `AT1.35.3`, `AT4.35.1` to `AT4.36.1`, `AT4.105.1` to `AT4.108.2` | Stirner's sentences set beside the authors' "corrected" versions in two columns. Read here left column first; the modern draft should make the pairing clear, perhaps with a note naming the matching verse. Flagged `review: true`. |
| `AT2.6.1` to `AT2.45.1` | The authors' table of Stirner's book, with "Neger", "Mongole" and "Kaukasier" as Stirner's stages of history. These are Stirner's categories, borrowed from Hegel, and Marx and Engels mock them; the words are of their time. Translate them plainly with a note, do not soften them, and do not let the mockery read as endorsement. |
| `AT3.13.3` to `AT3.28.3` | Greek. The Symbol-font Greek on mlwerke.de was restored by hand (see SOURCES.md); the phrases from Aristotle in AT3.28.3 should be checked against a printed MEW 3 before the modern draft cites them. |
| `AT6.33.1` to `AT6.198.2` | "B) Der Kommunismus": Stirner on communism, with the authors' "logical constructions" and the equations of `AT6.75.1` to `AT6.121.1` (tables in MEW, read row by row). The equations parody Stirner's method; keep them readable, one equation chain to a verse. |
| `NT3.1.1` to `NT3.1.4` | The prologue of John's Gospel, used as the opening of a chapter on Stirner's "logic". Use a standard public-domain English Bible wording (the King James) for the quoted verses so the parody is recognisable. |
| `NT5A.8.1` to `NT5B.240.1` | The long run of "equations" and "antitheses" on right, law, crime, property and the state. Many verses are one line of an equation. Decide once how to show "=" and the antithesis dash in the modern draft, and apply it everywhere. |
| `NT5B.186.2` to `NT5B.186.3` | ὁ θεὸς ἀγάπη ἐστίν (1 John 4:16) against Ἔρως: Stirner's slide from Christian love to sexual love. Keep the Greek with MEW's German gloss and give the English. |
| `NT5B.216.1` to `NT5B.231.1` | Stirner's dialogue between the landowner and the farm labourers, with the authors' comment after it. Speakers are part of the paragraphs here; keep it as dialogue. |
| `NT6.1.1` to `NT6.2.1` | Camões, *Os Lusíadas*, canto I, in Portuguese with MEW's German verse rendering in angle brackets (footnote `NT6.N1`). In NT6.1.2 Camões's nymphs of the Tagus ("Tágides minhas") have become nymphs of the Spree ("Spreïdes minhas"): the authors' joke on the Berlin philosopher, not a slip. Keep the Portuguese and say so in a note. |
| `AK.33.1` and `SLK.2.1` to `SLK.19.2` | Spanish titles in the manner of Cervantes, and the closing dialogue of the "Leipzig Council". Speaker names are headings in MEW (Sankt Sancho, Sankt Bruno); keep them as speaker labels. |
| `WS.1.1` to `WS.4.4` | The authors' definition of "true socialism". The Manifesto's section on "Der deutsche oder der 'wahre' Sozialismus" (CM III) is the short version; cross-cite in `notes`. |
| `WSI.127.1` to `WSI.146.1` and `WSIV.295.1` to `WSIV.331.4` | Two more sets of parallel columns: the "Bausteine" of the Rheinische Jahrbücher side by side, and Grün beside the Cabet he copied, with Cabet's French and the authors' German renderings. Flagged `review: true`. |
| `WSIV.300.1` to `WSIV.331.4` | Long French quotations from Cabet. MEW gives German renderings in angle brackets for some, not all. The modern draft translates the French; mark where the authors' own rendering differs. |
| `AT6.7.1`, `AT6.191.2` and other uses of "Schacher" | The vocabulary of petty trading ("Schacher", "Schachergeist") carried an anti-Jewish charge in 1840s German. Translate the sense exactly and say in a note what the word carried; do not hide it and do not add to it. |
