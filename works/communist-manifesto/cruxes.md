# Cruxes: where to start the arguments

Passages where the translation choice is hard, where the 1888 English departs from the German, or where readers from different tendencies take the same words in different directions. Each one is a good first Discussion. Title the thread with the verse number.

Terms that recur across many verses are handled in `glossary/terms.yaml` instead.

## Translation problems

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `0.1.2` | German says a holy *hunt* (Hetzjagd); Moore says "holy alliance to exorcise". |
| `I.1.1` + `N2` | "All hitherto existing society" is narrowed by Engels in 1888 to written history. How much of the note belongs in the main text for a modern reader? |
| `I.12.3` | *Staatsgewalt* is state power, not "the executive". The sentence is quoted constantly, so the change will be noticed. |
| `I.18.5` | "All that is solid melts into air" is a free rendering. The German speaks of everything belonging to the feudal estates evaporating. Keep the famous line or restore the image? |
| `I.23.2` | *Idiotismus* is isolation, not idiocy (Draper). |
| `I.49.1` | Moore replaced *Lumpenproletariat* with "dangerous class". |
| `II.67.1` | Moore and Engels added "necessitate further inroads upon the old social order", absent from the German. |
| `IV.12.1` | The closing line. Proletarians, working men, or workers; countries or world. |

## Where readings divide

These are places where the words are reasonably clear but what they commit a reader to is argued over. The `modern` field should translate faithfully; the disagreement goes into `readings` on the verse.

| Verse | Why it divides readers |
|---|---|
| `II.65.1` | "Raise the proletariat to ruling class" set beside "winning democracy". |
| `II.66.1` with `P1872.2.6` | Production centralised "in the hands of the State, i.e. of the proletariat organised as the ruling class", read against the 1872 lesson of the Commune that the working class cannot simply take over the ready-made state machinery. State-socialist, council-communist and anarchist readings differ sharply here. |
| `II.67.1` to `II.79.1` | The ten measures. In 1872 (`P1872.2.4`) the authors said this passage would be worded very differently and laid no special stress on it. |
| `II.80.1` to `II.81.1` | Public power losing its political character, and the association in which the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all. |
| `II.13.1`, `II.39.1` | *Aufhebung* of private property and of the family: abolish, transcend, or both? |
| `II.46.1` to `II.51.3` | The "community of women" passage answers an accusation. Modern readers often mistake whose voice is speaking. |
| `II.52.2` | "The workers have no country", next to `II.52.4`, which says the proletariat must first constitute itself as the nation. |
| `II.61.1` to `II.63.1` | The charge that communism abolishes religion and morality is put in the mouth of an objector (`II.61.1` to `II.61.4`); the authors' reply follows in `II.62` and `II.63`. A faithful translation keeps the quotation marks clear. Religious socialist readings of this passage belong in `readings`. |
| `I.22.1` to `I.23.3` | Bourgeois expansion described in the vocabulary of "barbarian" and "civilised" nations, "the East on the West". Anti-colonial readings of this passage belong in `notes` and `readings`. |
| `IV.11.2` | *gewaltsamen Umsturz*: forcible or violent overthrow. |
