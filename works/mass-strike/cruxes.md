# Cruxes in The Mass Strike, the Political Party and the Trade Unions

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[MS IV.14.19] revolutions cannot be schoolmastered`.

| Verse | Question |
|---|---|
| `I.2.1` to `I.4.17` | Luxemburg opens with Engels against the Bakuninist general strike (1873) and then says the Russian revolution has changed the question. How far does she revise Engels, and how far does she only say the conditions changed? The anarchist reading of these verses belongs in `commentary/`. |
| `II.2.7` | "kein historischer Materialismus im Sinne von Marx, sondern ein polizeilicher Materialismus". The pun needs a rendering that keeps both halves parallel. |
| `II.4.5` | Luxemburg puts her opponents' verbs for how a mass strike comes about in quotation marks. The marks carry the argument; keep them. |
| `IV.2.5` | The long list of every kind of strike. A modern draft may want to break it up; the verse is one sentence and should stay one verse. |
| `IV.2.11` | "die Bewegungsweise der proletarischen Masse": the mode of motion, the way the mass moves. The best-known phrase of the pamphlet. |
| `IV.14.8` to `IV.14.19` | What the party can and cannot lead, ending in "weil sich Revolutionen nicht schulmeistern lassen". Readers have taken this as a theory of spontaneity against organisation; others read it as a demand that the party lead politically. The translation should not settle that dispute. |
| `IV.16.1` to `IV.16.4` | Political leadership against "technical" leadership. See the glossary entry `Leitung`. |
| `V.4.6` | "Mit Paupers werden keine Revolutionen ... gemacht": the Russian worker is the equal of the West European. "Pauper" is an English loan word in the German; decide whether to keep it. |
| `VI.6.1` to `VI.7.15` | Against overrating organisation: the unorganised will be the most radical element. Compare `CM I` on the proletariat as a whole. |
| `VII.7.3` to `VII.7.5` | Defending the Reichstag franchise by a mass strike, and the jab at "Genosse Elm in Jena". Needs a footnote on the Jena congress of 1905 and its resolution (`II.8.1` to `II.8.6`). |
| `VIII.14.1` to `VIII.15.16` | Union officials and their "Bureaukratismus". Later read as an early analysis of labour bureaucracy. Keep her tone, which is sharp but not contemptuous of the members. |
| `VIII.19.3` | The mass as "der handelnde Chorus", the leaderships only "sprechende Personen". The image comes from Greek drama; keep it rather than flatten it to "the masses act". |
| `VIII.20.3` | "Und was sie ist, das wage sie zu scheinen." A quoted line of verse, probably Schiller; find the source, give it in a note and translate it as verse. |
| `VIII.N4` | The Mainz resolution, quoted in full. It is not Luxemburg's text but her opponents'. The modern draft should keep it recognisably a resolution, in a flatter register than hers. |
