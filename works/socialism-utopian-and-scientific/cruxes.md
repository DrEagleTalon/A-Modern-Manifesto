# Cruxes in Socialism: Utopian and Scientific

Good first Discussions. Title the thread with the citation, for example `[SUS III.25.12] withers away`.

| Verse | Problem |
|---|---|
| `III.25.9` to `III.25.13` | The state "dies out". The proletariat takes state power, and in doing so ends itself as a class and the state as state. Every tradition in the library reads this passage differently. Settle the wording here (see the glossary entry) and put the readings in `commentary/`. |
| `III.25.13` | The "free people's state" (freier Volksstaat) and the anarchist demand to abolish the state "overnight". Engels names both; the second is a direct reply to Bakunin's followers. A note should say who is meant without taking a side in the translation. |
| `III.7.12` to `III.8.10` | Social production against capitalist appropriation, the core contradiction. Hard to make plain without losing the logic. |
| `III.18.1` to `III.22.9` | Joint-stock companies, trusts and state ownership. Engels says state ownership is not socialism (see also `III.N4`, the note on Bismarck and the "spurious Socialism" of state monopolies). Readers will bring modern nationalisation to this; keep Engels's examples and leave the parallels for adaptations. |
| `III.28.10` | "The leap from the realm of necessity into the realm of freedom". Aveling's "ascent of man" is a loose rendering. |
| `III.29.1` to `III.32.6` | Engels's numbered summary. Its outline form (I, II A to D, III) should survive in the modern draft. |
| `II.3.1` to `II.4.4` | Metaphysics against dialectics. "Metaphysical" here means thinking in fixed, separate categories, not the modern sense of the word. Needs a note at `II.3.1`. |
| `II.3.2` | "Seine Rede ist ja, ja, nein, nein" quotes Matthew 5:37. Aveling gives the King James wording. Keep the quotation recognisable. |
| `I.4.3` to `I.4.7` | The early communist currents (Münzer, the Levellers, Babeuf, Morelly, Mably). Names a modern reader will not know; notes, not changes to the text. |
| `I.15.5` | MEW reads "Allianz den Franzosen von 1815 predigen mit den Siegern von Waterloo". The word order is odd and may be a transcription slip; check against a printed MEW 19 before translating. |
| `I.20.1` to `I.22.2` | Owen's figures (2,500 workers doing the work of 600,000; £300,000 profit). The German converts pounds to marks; the English does not. Leave money conversion to the adaptation layer. |
| `P1880` | Marx's preface is known only through MEW's German translation here. Translate from the French when it is added; until then mark the modern draft as a translation of a translation. |
| `P1892` | Engels wrote it in English, so the modern draft is a modernisation, not a translation. "Middle-class" means bourgeoisie throughout. His remarks on religion and on English "respectability" are long; resist cutting them. |

## Alignment notes

Aveling splits long German sentences often, so most review flags are `1:2` and `1:3` matches. Check that each English sentence sits on the German verse it translates. The ingest already repaired three places (`I.6.1`, `I.16.9`, `III.10.8`); see SOURCES.md.
