# Adaptations

Short modern pamphlets built on the works in this library. A translation tells you what the author said. An adaptation explains it with today's paychecks, rents, warehouses and phones. Both are needed, and they must never be confused.

## The rules

1. **Say what it is.** Every adaptation opens with a line naming the work it adapts and saying that it is a modern adaptation, not a translation.
2. **Cite your verses.** Every claim taken from the original carries a citation in the form `WLC I.13.1` or `CM II.66.1`. `scripts/validate.py` fails if a citation points at a verse that does not exist.
3. **Keep the author's argument.** You may add examples, numbers and explanations. You may not make the author argue something they did not argue. When you go beyond the original (new data, later theory, your own conclusion), mark it as yours.
4. **Mark the disagreements.** Where the traditions in this library disagree (Marxist, anarchist, syndicalist, anti-colonial, religious socialist), say so instead of picking a side silently.
5. **Write for one reader.** Someone at a union meeting, a food distribution or a break room, who has never read theory. Target 15-40 pages, 30-45 minutes of reading.
